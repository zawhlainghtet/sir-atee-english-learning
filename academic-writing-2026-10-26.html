First 10 Minutes — Normal Reading

Model Essay 1 · Advantages and Disadvantages

Four-Day Working Week

Some companies are introducing a four-day working week without reducing employees’ salaries. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this development?

A growing number of employers are allowing staff to complete their weekly duties in four days while continuing to receive full pay. This arrangement may improve employees’ well-being and even raise productivity. Nevertheless, it can also create scheduling difficulties and place greater pressure on workers. Its success therefore depends largely on the nature of the organisation and the way the policy is introduced.

The principal advantage is a healthier balance between work and personal life. An additional free day gives employees more time for family responsibilities, exercise, rest or further study. As a result, they may return to work with greater energy and concentration. A shorter week can also encourage organisations to operate more efficiently. When time is limited, managers are more likely to remove unnecessary meetings, simplify approval procedures and focus on measurable results rather than hours spent at a desk. If the same output can be achieved in fewer days, both staff and employers may benefit.

However, this model is not equally suitable for every workplace. Hospitals, shops and transport services must often operate throughout the week, so reducing each employee’s working days may require additional recruitment. This could increase labour costs even if individual salaries remain unchanged. Moreover, some organisations may compress five days of work into four longer and more intense days. Employees would then gain an extra day off but might experience exhaustion during the rest of the week. The policy could therefore damage well-being if workloads are not reduced or redesigned.

In conclusion, a four-day week can offer better work–life balance and motivate companies to use time more effectively. Its main risks are higher staffing costs and excessive daily pressure. Before adopting the system, employers should test it in a limited trial, measure both output and staff health, and adapt workloads to realistic working hours.

Model Essay 2 · Two-Part Question

Public Libraries in the Digital Age

Many people now obtain information online instead of visiting public libraries. Why has this change occurred? What role should public libraries play in the future?

Digital technology has changed how people search for information, and online sources have replaced many routine visits to public libraries. This shift has occurred mainly because internet resources are fast, convenient and continuously available. However, libraries should not disappear; instead, they should evolve into inclusive centres for reliable information, digital access and community learning.

The most obvious cause of the change is convenience. A student can search thousands of articles from home within minutes, whereas travelling to a library takes time and may be limited by opening hours. Online materials are also easy to update, which makes them attractive in rapidly changing fields such as technology and medicine. In addition, smartphones have made information available almost everywhere. Even when online sources vary in quality, many users choose speed over careful evaluation because it helps them complete an immediate task.

In the future, libraries should help people benefit from digital information without being harmed by its weaknesses. Librarians can teach users how to identify trustworthy evidence, compare sources and recognise misleading content. This role is increasingly important because access to more information does not automatically produce better understanding. Libraries should also provide computers, internet connections and quiet study areas for people who cannot afford them at home. By doing so, they can reduce the educational gap between wealthier citizens and those with fewer resources.

Finally, public libraries can function as shared learning spaces rather than simply as buildings that store books. They might host language classes, technical workshops, children’s activities and talks by local experts. Such services create face-to-face opportunities that an online search cannot replace.

In conclusion, people have moved online because digital information is quick, current and easy to reach. Libraries should respond by developing information-literacy education, equal access to technology and community programmes, while continuing to preserve high-quality reading materials.

Next 10 Minutes — Concise Analysis

Essay 1 Analysis · About 5 minutes

Why This Structure Is Used

Introduction

  • Sentence 1 paraphrases the task. Sentence 2 previews the benefits. Sentence 3 begins with “Nevertheless” and previews the drawbacks.
  • Sentence 4 gives the controlling judgement: the policy is conditional, not universally good or bad. This prepares the reader for a balanced essay.

Advantages Paragraph

  • The topic sentence gives the human benefit first. The next sentence explains how the free day is used.
  • “As a result” links rest to improved energy; then “also” moves from the employee benefit to the organisational benefit.
  • The paragraph finishes with a cautious conditional: If + present, may + verb.

extra personal time → better recovery → stronger concentration
less available time → fewer inefficient activities → equal output

Disadvantages Paragraph

  • “However” marks the change of direction. A service-sector example then proves that the model cannot fit every workplace.
  • “Moreover” adds a separate risk: work may be compressed instead of reduced.
  • “Would… but might…” contrasts a certain-looking benefit with a possible negative consequence.

Conclusion and Useful Grammar

  • The conclusion summarises both sides, then gives a practical three-part recommendation. It adds no new argument.
  • while + -ing: “while continuing to receive full pay” describes two simultaneous conditions.
  • When + passive: “When time is limited…” creates a general cause-and-effect statement.
  • if + passive negative: “if workloads are not reduced…” states the condition behind the warning.
Essay 2 Analysis · About 5 minutes

Why This Structure Is Used

Introduction

  • Sentence 1 introduces the trend. Sentence 2 answers why. Sentence 3 answers the future-role question.
  • The semicolon in “should not disappear; instead…” joins two closely related complete ideas and sharply redirects the argument.

Cause Paragraph

  • The topic sentence names convenience as the main cause. The comparison with travelling explains it concretely.
  • “Also” adds a second reason, up-to-date content; “In addition” adds a third, mobile access.
  • “Even when” concedes that quality is uneven before explaining why users still choose online sources.

Future-Role Paragraphs

  • The first future paragraph responds directly to online weaknesses: librarians teach evaluation, while libraries provide access for those without equipment.
  • “This role” refers back to information-literacy teaching, so the argument progresses instead of restarting.
  • “By doing so” refers to providing technology and space, then explains the social result.
  • The short final body paragraph adds the community role. “Rather than” replaces the old storage-only image with a broader purpose.

online convenience → fewer library visits → new library roles → fairer access and stronger evaluation skills

Conclusion and Useful Grammar

  • The conclusion gives one sentence to each task question, ensuring both are fully answered.
  • whereas: contrasts two situations: online searching and physical travel.
  • who + clause: “people who cannot afford them” precisely defines the group.
  • because + clause: explains why the role matters without creating a new topic.
  • while + -ing: adds the continuing duty of preserving quality materials.
